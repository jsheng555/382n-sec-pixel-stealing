\section{Conclusion}

In this midpoint report, we described our progress so far in replicating the pixel stealing attack by Andrysco et al. ~\cite{andrysco2015subnormal}. Measuring floating-point timing across eleven ARM and x86 processors, we found that the subnormal multiply penalty is still present on modern Intel processors, roughly 30 times slower than normal multiplication, but mostly gone on AMD Zen 3, Apple silicon, and NVIDIA Grace, so we chose the Intel Xeon 5218 as our target.

We also built the setup for the end-to-end attack: a cross-origin victim page with a known secret image, and an attacker page that confirms the same-origin policy is enforced, enlarges individual victim pixels, and measures frame render times. For the rest of the project, we plan to drive the subnormal filter through a browser that runs it on the CPU and measure how accurately we can recover the secret image. Whether or not the attack works on a modern browser, we expect our results to show how hardware timing behavior and browser defenses have changed since the original attack was published.
