\section{Conclusion}

In this midpoint report, we described our progress so far in replicating the pixel stealing attack by Andrysco et al. ~\cite{andrysco2015subnormal}. We first measured how long floating-point addition and multiplication take on normal and subnormal operands across eight ARM and x86 processors. We found that the subnormal timing difference is still present on modern Intel processors, where subnormal multiplication is roughly 30 times slower than normal multiplication, but is mostly gone on AMD Zen 3, Apple silicon, and NVIDIA Grace. Based on these results, we chose the Intel Xeon 5218 as the target for our attack.

We also built the setup for the end-to-end attack. This includes a cross-origin victim page with a known secret image, and an attacker page that confirms the same-origin policy is enforced, enlarges individual victim pixels, and measures how long frames take to render. For the rest of the project, we plan to apply an SVG filter that creates subnormal values, find a browser setup where that filter runs on the CPU, and measure how accurately we can recover the secret image. Whether or not the attack works on a modern browser, we expect our results to show how hardware timing behavior and browser defenses have changed since the original attack was published.
