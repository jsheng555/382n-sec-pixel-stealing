\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Browsers are designed around the same-origin policy, which
prevents one website from directly inspecting the contents
of another website. Despite this isolation, webpages still
share underlying resources such as the CPU, GPU, caches,
and browser rendering system. Differences in how these
resources behave while processing particular visual content
can potentially leak information through a side channel.

There are various methods to leak pixel information, including exploitation of software timing differences ~\cite{stone2013pixel}, hardware timing differences ~\cite{andrysco2015subnormal}, GPU compression ~\cite{wang2024gpuzip}, and microarchitectural cache state ~\cite{oconnell2024pixelthief}. Our initial approach is to exploit hardware timing differences of subnormal floating point computation. We are also exploring GPU.zip as an alternative leaking method to subnormal timing.
